How to use this document

Three parts, then they own the method

Weeks 1–3 build the instrument. Weeks 4–7 stretch the same marks across threshold, time, learning, and memory. Weeks 8–12 turn one borrowed computation into a pair project. Students do not invent a new paradigm in November. They restage something the room already knows how to measure.

Lab notes (weeks 1–9): phenomenon, prediction, number, one limitation. 400 words. Due Tuesday 17:00. Drop the lowest. Two critiques are graded in the room (week 8 and week 12). The pair project is 40%.

Week 1 · Wednesday 2 September 2026
playground.html

How a page computes

page = HTML + CSS + JavaScript

First night after add/drop chaos. No theory yet. The goal is that nobody is afraid of View Source, and that the three web layers are already named as structure, appearance, and behavior of an experiment.

18:00 Frame
A webpage is already a three-part model of a trial. Names, not syntax.
18:25 Demo
Change one layer at a time on the playground. Students call which layer moved.
18:40 Make
Edit heading, color, and the click so it reports a millisecond time.
19:25 Compute
Write one sentence: which layer did the work in your change?
20:05 Remix
Arts: make the card a composition you would hang. Science: make the button collect two RTs and show the difference.
Read before
Nothing. Bring a laptop that can open a browser.

Lab note: screenshot one change and name the layer. What would this page need before it could be an experiment?

Week 2 · Wednesday 9 September 2026
search.html

Signature lab · visual search

RT = a + bN

Feature Integration Theory as a slope. Feature search should stay near 0 ms/item. Conjunction search should cost time as the field grows. This is the computation the rest of the term refers back to.

18:00 Frame
Pop-out versus binding. Draw the two predicted lines on the board before anyone clicks.
18:25 Demo
Instructor illustration data only (about 1 vs 25 ms/item). Then a 6-trial click demo. Do not interpret the demo slopes.
18:40 Make
Full 36-trial block. Students are the subjects.
19:25 Compute
Read both slopes. One sentence: did your mind match the figure?
20:05 Remix
Arts: change palette and ground, rerun a short block, ask if beauty helped or hid the T. Science: propose a target-absent key or a third set size.
Read before
Treisman & Gelade (1980), first four pages and Figure 1. Wolfe (2020) “Guided Search 6.0” abstract only is enough if the paper feels long.

Lab note: your two slopes, the prediction you wrote at 18:10, and one reason a slope can lie (practice, lapses, grouping).

Week 3 · Wednesday 16 September 2026
sdt.html

From how fast to how separable

d′ = z(Hit) − z(False alarm)

Percent correct is not a grade for a mind. Sensitivity and saying yes come apart. Arts students meet this as discrimination in a noisy field. Science students meet it as the 300-level move past accuracy.

18:00 Frame
Two overlapping clouds. Criterion is a policy, not a talent.
18:25 Demo
Hold d′ still and slide criterion. Watch percent correct change while sensitivity does not.
18:40 Make
20-trial faint-T field. J = yes, F = no. Compute your own d′ and c.
19:25 Compute
Compare slider-world to your body. Are you liberal or conservative tonight?
20:05 Remix
Arts: redesign the noise field so the T is a mark in a composition. Science: add a second contrast and predict which d′ moves.
Read before
Stanislaw & Todorov (1999), “Calculation of signal detection theory measures,” the worked example. Skip the software appendix.

Lab note: your d′ and c. A sentence on what would happen to “accuracy” if you just said yes more often.

Week 4 · Wednesday 23 September 2026
page to build: staircase.html

Thresholds on the same marks

next contrast = last contrast ± step · (correct ? −1 : +1)

A 1-up/1-down staircase on the red T. The computation is a rule that chases the edge of seeing. Reuses Week 2–3 stimuli so the room is not learning a new alphabet.

18:00 Frame
Threshold as a moving target, not a score. Why 70.7% and 1-up/1-down are different rules.
18:25 Demo
Watch one staircase climb and settle. Call the reversal.
18:40 Make
Run 40 trials. Plot contrast over trial. Mean of the last six reversals.
19:25 Compute
Report your threshold. Did the walk look like a hunt or a wander?
20:05 Remix
Arts: change the ground so the threshold should move. Science: switch to 2-down/1-up and predict the new equilibrium.
Read before
Kingdom & Prins, Psychophysics, the short staircase chapter, or Levitt (1971) first three pages.

Lab note: threshold, rule used, and whether a different ground would have changed the number. Due 29 Sep — there is no class on the 30th.

Week 5 · Wednesday 7 October 2026
page to build: blink.html

Time as a medium

P(T2 correct | T1 correct) = f(lag)

RSVP stream, two targets, a mini attentional blink. The computation is a function of lag, not a single mean. This is the first night the Arts half of the room can treat time as material.

18:00 Frame
Why a second target vanishes around 200–500 ms. Draw the U-shaped lag function.
18:25 Demo
One slow stream, one real stream. Students feel the miss before they plot it.
18:40 Make
Lags 1, 3, 8. Enough trials for a sketch, not a paper.
19:25 Compute
Three points. Is there a blink, a lag-1 sparing, or a tired evening?
20:05 Remix
Arts: restyle the stream as a film strip or score. Science: add T1-absent catch trials and say what they are for.
Read before
Raymond, Shapiro, & Arnell (1992), abstract plus Figure 1. One page of discussion.

Lab note: your three lag points and whether you believe the dip. Thanksgiving is Monday; the note is still due Tuesday 13 Oct.

Week 6 · Wednesday 14 October 2026
page to build: rw.html

Learning as prediction error

ΔV = α (λ − V)

Rescorla–Wagner in the browser. Students slide α, watch V chase λ, then break the model with blocking. No rats. The plot is the animal.

18:00 Frame
Learning is error, not pairing. Why surprising outcomes change V and expected ones do not.
18:25 Demo
Acquisition, extinction, and a blocking design on the same sliders.
18:40 Make
Fit α to a short made-up acquisition curve, then predict extinction.
19:25 Compute
Show the residual. Where does RW refuse to learn what a person would?
20:05 Remix
Arts: stage V as brightness, pitch, or a growing mark. Science: add a second cue and run blocking.
Read before
Rescorla (1988), “Pavlovian conditioning: It’s not what you think it is,” or a one-page RW primer from the instructor.

Lab note: your α, one plot, and one failure of the model you actually saw on screen.

Week 7 · Wednesday 21 October 2026
page to build: memory.html

Memory as a curve, not a story

P(recall) = f(serial position)

Immediate serial recall of letters or marks. Primacy, recency, and span become plots. Last content computation before the room turns toward making.

18:00 Frame
Why the ends of a list live differently. Span is a number; the curve is the theory.
18:25 Demo
One list spoken, one list on screen. Collect the room’s recency by hand.
18:40 Make
Computer-paced lists. Plot position 1–7. Optional: filled delay to kill recency.
19:25 Compute
Is your recency a property of the mind or of the last item still ringing?
20:05 Remix
Arts: replace letters with a sequence of images you designed. Science: add a 10-second filled delay and predict which end of the curve dies.
Read before
Murdock (1962) Figure 1, or Glanzer & Cunitz (1966) on the two ends of the list. Skim.

Lab note: your serial-position plot and one sentence naming which end you trust.

Week 8 · Wednesday 28 October 2026
graded in-class critique 1

Restage one computation

same math · new medium

No new equation. Pairs pick a computation from weeks 2–7 and restage it as an artwork, instrument, or game that still produces the number. This is the project seed. The critique is the grade.

18:00 Frame
What must stay for it to still be the computation. What may change.
18:25 Demo
Instructor restages search or d′ in two minutes as a bad example and a good one.
18:40 Make
Pairs choose and build a sketch in the browser. Steal from existing pages.
19:25 Compute
Does the sketch still emit a slope, a d′, a threshold, a lag function, a V, or a curve?
20:05 Remix / critique
Four-minute pair showings. Room asks only: what is the number, and can a classmate who missed that week still see it?
Read before
Bring a one-paragraph proposal. No additional article.

Lab note: which computation you restaged, the number it still produces, and what you will keep for the project.

Week 9 · Wednesday 4 November 2026
last weekly lab note

Uncertainty, and what a slope cannot claim

CI = M ± t · s / √n

Reopen Week 2 CSVs. Error bars, practice effects, outliers we already drop (150–2500 ms). The point is not a new test. It is to stop overclaiming.

18:00 Frame
A slope without a cloud is a posture. What n = 6 per cell can and cannot say.
18:25 Demo
Add interval bands to the search chart. Watch a “classic pattern” become “maybe.”
18:40 Make
Students replot their own Week 2 export. Split first half vs second half for practice.
19:25 Compute
Rewrite your Week 2 sentence with the interval in it.
20:05 Remix
Arts: visualize uncertainty as material, not a whisker. Science: say what sample size would make you trust a 5 ms/item difference.
Read before
Cumming (2014), “The new statistics,” first four pages, or a one-page CI primer.

Lab note: the rewritten Week 2 claim. This is the last weekly note. Use Reading Week to draft the project proposal.
