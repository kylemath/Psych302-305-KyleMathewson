Attention architecture
Networks & brains

A curated collection for comparing attention architecture in artificial neural networks with biological selection, routing, gating, and memory. Curated 14 October 2026.

10 cross-domain bridges · 10 biological foundations · 10 machine architectures

Start with these eight

  1. Lindsay (2020) — Vocabulary and scope
  2. Lindsay (2018) — Gain changes in a functioning visual model
  3. Reynolds (2009) — The biological normalization computation
  4. Vaswani (2017) — The transformer operation
  5. Ramsauer (2021) — Attention as associative retrieval
  6. Whittington (2022) — A hippocampal architecture correspondence
  7. Salehi (2026) — Recurrent selection and binding
  8. König (2026) — A current cortical microcircuit hypothesis

Compare operations, then mechanisms

Selection: What information gets prioritized?

Routing: Which populations or representations communicate?

Implementation: Gain, inhibition, recurrence, synaptic memory, or oscillatory timing?

A shared function or mathematical update does not establish a shared biological implementation. For an EEG route, start with papers 12 and 18–20.

Selection and access

“Top” means editorial priority based on direct relevance, foundational influence, and mechanism coverage—not a citation-count ranking or an exhaustive systematic review. Ten bridge papers are followed by ten biological foundations and ten machine architectures. Recent 2026 entries are included for relevance rather than accumulated influence. Entries were checked against primary publisher, proceedings, repository, or author-hosted records; annotations synthesize accessible abstracts and article text. Cross-domain interpretations are labeled separately. This is a linked reading library, not a bundle of downloaded paper PDFs. Some publisher full texts require institutional access. Conference years are used where verified; preprints are identified explicitly.

The connection and limitation paragraphs are curator annotations, not author abstracts. Source links accompany each entry. Full-text route means an open version when listed, otherwise a publisher or repository route; access conditions vary.
