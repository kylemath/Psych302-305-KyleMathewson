What counts as a project

Anything you can work on with code and that connects to psychology or to how people think and behave. Some shapes it can take: a small study on yourself (reaction time, a questionnaire, a two-condition comparison, a browser game like game.html); a reanalysis of data you already have or of an open dataset; a tool, demo, or visualization; or a written methods-and-results page. Start from something we did in class or go beyond it. New libraries and languages are fine if you can explain what they do. Big or small is your call.

What you do not need

A finished product, a result that matches your prediction, a partner, classmates’ data, or statistics you cannot explain. You are your own participant unless you arrange otherwise. If you want data from classmates, ask the instructor first; the data stay in the room.

Score breakdown

4 points · The idea
What you want to make, find out, or try, and why it interests you. Clear enough that someone else could explain it back to you.
4 points · The approach
How you will go about it: what you will build or do, with what tools, and what data (if any). If you expect a result, say what you expect before you look.
4 points · Steps
Your first step (before Week 10), what “some progress” would look like by 2 December, and a stretch goal if things go well.
3 points · Starting point and risk
What you already have (files, data, skills) and one thing that could get in the way, with a fallback.
Required, not scored · AI note
What you asked an AI tool, what you kept, and what you changed, or no Copilot tonight.

Plans are allowed to change later. You are marked on how clear and workable this plan is, not on how ambitious it is. If you miss class on 28 October for a reason the instructor accepts, hand the plan in by Friday 30 October, 17:00.

Part A · Your work and FINAL.md · 30 points (30%)

10 points · Evidence of progress
Real work pushed to week10-project/ since the plan: code, a page, data, figures, or notes. It does not have to be finished or even run yet, as long as the files show what you did.
7 points · What you did
FINAL.md says what you planned, what you actually did, and where the project stands now, with links to your files.
6 points · Show something
At least one concrete thing you can point to: a figure, a result, a screenshot, a working demo, or an example output, with a sentence on what it shows.
5 points · What changed and what is next
How and why the plan changed, what did not work, and what you would do next if you kept going.
2 points · AI note
What you asked, kept, and changed, or no Copilot tonight.

Part B · In-class talk · 5 points (5%)

On Wednesday 2 December, show the class what you have in 3 minutes: what you set out to do, what you built, one thing you learned, and what comes next. Show your files, page, or FINAL.md; no slides needed. Everyone presents.

If you miss the talk for a reason the instructor accepts, present by Friday 4 December, 17:00. If you hand in neither the report nor the talk, the Faculty process for a missed final assessment applies.

Not marked: whether the project is finished, how big it is, whether your prediction came true, or how polished it looks. A small project reported honestly can earn full marks.
